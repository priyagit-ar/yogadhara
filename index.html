A class, step by step

The shape of a class

Every Sivananda class follows the same order, one that has hardly changed in more than sixty years. That is the point: once your body knows what comes next, you stop following and start practising. Gentler variations are offered all the way through, so beginners and experienced students practise side by side.

  1. Arrive śavāsana

    Lie down and let the day fall away. A few quiet minutes, then a short chant to begin.

  2. Breathe prāṇāyāma

    Kapālabhāti to clear and energise, then alternate-nostril breathing to steady the mind.

  3. Warm up sūrya namaskāra

    Rounds of sun salutation, moving with the breath, until you’re warm from head to toe.

  4. Postures āsana

    The twelve basic postures, from headstand to triangle, each held for a few slow breaths, with rest in between.

  5. Deep relaxation śavāsana

    Back where you began: a long, guided relaxation until the whole body lets go. A short chant to close.

Each class lasts about [90] minutes, from the first breath to the last.

The twelve basic postures, in order

  1. Headstandśīrṣāsana
  2. Shoulderstandsarvāṅgāsana
  3. Ploughhalāsana
  4. Fishmatsyāsana
  5. Sitting forward bendpaścimottānāsana
  6. Cobrabhujaṅgāsana
  7. Locustśalabhāsana
  8. Bowdhanurāsana
  9. Half spinal twistardha matsyendrāsana
  10. Crow or peacockkākāsana, mayūrāsana
  11. Standing forward bendpāda hastāsana
  12. Triangletrikoṇāsana

Every posture has gentler stages, and you’ll be shown the one that suits you today. Headstand, for example, is built up gradually, over as many weeks as it takes.

A long time. Without a break. With devotion. Notice what isn’t on the list: talent, flexibility, the perfect pose. Yoga asks for a steady stream, a dhārā, not a flood. A weekly class is the simplest way to keep it flowing.

Your teacher

Meet Priya

Yoga has been a quiet and constant presence in my life since childhood. I grew up watching my father practise regularly, and yoga has always felt familiar, something that was part of me even before I fully understood it.

Over the years, my connection with yoga stayed personal. Through different phases of life I kept practising at home, finding moments of stillness and grounding in my daily routine. It was my way of staying connected to myself amid life’s responsibilities.

Life also brought its share of challenges. After a deep personal loss, I found myself in a place of overwhelming grief and anxiety. During this time my connection to yoga deepened, not just as a physical practice, but as a source of stability, calm and inner strength.

Through breathwork and meditation, I slowly began to feel a powerful shift. Yoga helped quieten my mind and brought a sense of grounding I had not felt before. It opened a path inward, helping me reconnect with myself and slowly rebuild from within.

With time, I felt a strong inner calling to take this further. When my son left for university, I finally took the step and trained as a yoga teacher with Sivananda Yoga in India. It was not an easy decision, but it came from a place of readiness and deep inner alignment.

Today, my intention in teaching is simple and sincere. I don’t focus on weight loss or perfect postures. My approach is rooted in helping people find balance, inner strength and calm, through breath, gentle Hatha yoga and meditation.

My teaching style is gentle, structured and spiritual. I aim to create a space where people feel safe and supported, and can move at their own pace, without pressure or expectation.

I feel especially drawn to supporting people who are experiencing stress and mental overwhelm, whether from work, home life or the demands of everyday living. In today’s fast-paced world, many of us rarely pause. Through yoga, I hope to offer that pause: a moment of stillness, awareness and restoration.

What makes my teaching personal is the connection I build with each person. I see yoga not just as a practice, but as a shared experience: a space where we come together, breathe together and gently explore the journey inward.

Through Yoga Dhara, my intention is to guide others towards a more balanced, grounded and peaceful way of being.

Warmly,Priya

Rooted in the five points of yoga

Every class rests on the five points of yoga, as Swami Vishnudevananda distilled them:

  • Proper exercise āsana
  • Proper breathing prāṇāyāma
  • Proper relaxation śavāsana
  • Proper diet āhāra
  • Positive thinking and meditation vedānta, dhyāna

Every stream has a source

What Priya teaches has been handed down from teacher to student for almost a century. In Sanskrit, an unbroken line like this is called a paramparā.

  1. 1936 · Rishikesh

    On the banks of the Ganges, Swami Sivananda founds the Divine Life Society. He sums up his teaching in six words: serve, love, give, purify, meditate, realize.

  2. 1957 · To the West

    He sends his student Swami Vishnudevananda abroad with three words: “People are waiting.” Two years later, the first Sivananda Yoga Vedanta Centre opens in Montreal.

  3. [Year] · India

    Priya trains as a yoga teacher with Sivananda Yoga in India, after years of practising at home.

  4. Today · [City] and online

    The same practice, passed on as it was received, one class at a time.

FAQ

Before you come

I’ve never done yoga, and I’m not flexible. Is this for me?

Yes. Flexibility comes with practice; it isn’t an entry requirement. Every posture is taught step by step, with a gentler version always on offer. Just mention that you’re new when you book.

Is there chanting? Do I have to join in?

Each class opens and closes with a short chant, as is traditional in Sivananda yoga. Join in, hum along or simply listen: all three are welcome. You don’t need to know the words or hold any particular belief.

What do I need for an online class?

A mat, a blanket for the relaxation, comfortable clothes and enough floor to lie down with your arms stretched out. A laptop or tablet works better than a phone: set it a little way off, at floor level, so Priya can see all of you. And practise on an empty stomach; about two hours after a meal is ideal.

Online or in person: which should I choose?

It’s the same class either way. Online, there’s no travel and you practise in your own space. In person, you share the room and Priya is right there with you. [Class cards work for both, so you can mix.]

Where are the in-person classes?

In and around [City]. There’s no permanent studio yet, so each class is held at an announced location. Dates go up on Instagram, and you get the exact address on WhatsApp when you book.

Send Priya a WhatsApp message saying which class you’d like. She’ll confirm your place and send the payment details. No account, no app, no newsletter to unsubscribe from.
